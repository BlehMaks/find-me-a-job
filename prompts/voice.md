# Authorial voice setup

Ask for three to five texts the person wrote themselves, preferably a short professional message, a longer email and a text in each intended language. Record whether each was independently written, edited or AI-assisted. Do not infer personal authorship solely from a file date or detector. Samples stay in the private workspace.

Analyse observable choices: greeting/sign-off; formality; directness; sentence length; explanation length; vocabulary; punctuation; expressions of interest; requests and follow-ups. Distinguish deliberate voice from typos or habits that hinder communication. Save a short profile by language, channel and audience with sample references and confidence.

Show one concise initial contact and one reply in the proposed voice. Ask for approval/correction. If samples are unavailable, offer neutral professional language and calibrate from edits. Do not delay a truthful general CV solely for missing samples.

Use `skills/humanizer/SKILL.md` after content selection. Preserve facts, exact useful terms, recipient intent and professional boundaries. Keep draft → remaining-tells audit → final rewrite in private editorial notes even when the bundled upstream process uses another output shape. No casual anecdotes, first person or invented personality in a CV merely to sound human. Letters may use first person naturally. Match the person's voice while remaining slightly more polite than the counterpart.
