# Campaign briefing and approval

Start with the person's model of their search: “Describe how people in your field normally find opportunities and apply. Which sources, people, institutions, calls and documents have mattered to you?” Use their experience as the first input and research it against current authoritative sources.

Ask in focused rounds for desired duties, disliked tasks, professional direction/seniority, employment or research/program/grant/creative pathways, markets/languages, location/remote/travel/relocation, compensation where relevant, work-authorization constraints, timing, exclusions, confidentiality, outreach comfort, supporting-document permissions and resource limits. Separate hard limits from preferences. Show contradictions explicitly: for example a desired role whose core duty is rejected; propose adjacent titles or a clarified tradeoff rather than silently violating one side.

Research market/discipline norms using `markets/` as dated starters. Read live call/employer details. Propose a broadened title list with synonyms and adjacent roles, direct/transfer evidence, gaps and exclusions. Propose primary sources, public openings, LinkedIn/authorized email alerts and field-specific people/programme/call sources; explain useful additions the person did not name.

Obtain approval of the expanded **title/resource strategy**, hard/flexible filters, permissible expansion classes, outreach purposes/limits, visibility, supported submission routes and schedule before activation. Three to five alert titles are a useful starting set, not a fixed cap or universal need. New resources within approved classes may be explored autonomously; new commitments or scope conflicts need confirmation.

Record a versioned policy with effective date, source of each rule and supersession. Set one-off or scheduled runs, summary cadence, immediate meaningful-event notifications and a small task-tab budget. Review current capabilities and mandatory handoffs. A missing login should not remove a good opportunity; save it for recovery.

Human-reserved decisions: appointments, final compensation/terms, offers, withdrawal, paid actions and material new obligations. In managed mode campaign setting changes belong to the operator. Existing factual answers remain valid within their scope; policy changes do not rewrite submitted history.
