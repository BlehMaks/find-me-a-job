# Intake interview script

Use the user's chosen language. Explain: “We will reconstruct your experience from several sources, confirm what you personally did, and turn it into a reusable knowledge base and a general CV. You can answer in ordinary language, skip confidential names, and pause between projects.”

First ask for available CV versions from different dates, existing profile/portfolio/publications and the work areas the person wants to explore. Explain that documents and narratives are private; agent/provider access still processes the data under the configured service. Ask what may be disclosed publicly.

Build an inventory before interviewing. Show each experience with provisional dates, source disagreements and missing points. For each, ask only unanswered questions:

- What was the setting/problem, intended audience and constraint? Why did the work matter?
- Which parts did you personally own, decide, execute or support? What did others do?
- How did you work: methods, tools, research/creative process, partners and review?
- What was delivered? Was it a proposal, prototype, completed work, publication, production system or ongoing activity?
- What changed? Are there measured numbers, documented feedback, adoption, deadlines, funding or quality outcomes? What is the denominator/time period and who measured it? Approximate values remain labelled.
- What was difficult and what did you change? What result are you proud of that an earlier CV missed?
- Which names, data or outcomes must remain confidential? Can a neutral descriptor be used?

For science ask about independent contribution, methods, authorship, publication status, datasets, teaching and grants. For creative work ask about brief, concept, execution, credited role, portfolio rights and audience/client outcome. For industry ask about business/process context, responsibility, collaboration and practical outcome.

After each item, reflect back a factual summary and two or more distinct potential achievements where supported. Ask the person to correct ownership or implied expertise. Avoid generic praise; help recognize concrete achievement: “You did more than assist here if you chose the method and ran the analysis. Is that accurate?”

Save source-linked facts, unresolved contradictions and next questions. Complete the shortest useful remaining interview, not a repetitive full restart. End the intake with an inspectable KB, approved variants, recurring-answer setup and verified general CV; then move to campaign briefing.
