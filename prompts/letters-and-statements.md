# Letters, statements and proposals

Read actual call/form requirements before drafting. Tailor every required letter automatically. Optional industry/engineering letters require the person's request or explicit campaign policy; do not spend effort writing one by default. Academic correspondence can be central to the application and follows its own strategy.

For a cover letter select two or three strongest evidence-backed connections to the specific work, explain motivation from the person's confirmed interests and close with a clear request. Avoid repeating the CV, invented enthusiasm/history or generic company praise. Use approved voice and Humanizer, then factual/recipient/length checks.

For research/teaching statements first interview about the question, contribution, method/pedagogy, evidence, next steps and relevance to the institution. Separate completed research from proposed work. Distinguish published, accepted, submitted, under-review and in-preparation outputs. Check authorship/attribution and confidentiality. Read and cite the recipient's current work accurately; do not imply a relationship or endorsement.

For grants/programs extract eligibility, deadline/timezone, evaluation criteria, format, budget, ethics, host/partner commitments, referee process and all attachments. Prepare a requirement-to-evidence matrix. Ask the person to approve scientific ideas, feasibility, budget, originality/attribution and new obligations. Obtain any required host/partner confirmation. The agent may then submit under the approved procedure and tool permissions. Drafting is not approval and approval is not delivery evidence.

For creative calls collect the brief, credited role, portfolio rights, selection rationale and any requested artistic statement. Do not present collaborative work as sole authorship. Mandatory statements use the call's structure rather than a generic cover-letter template.

Record exact approved content/version and supporting files in the bundle. Before sending use the same identity, attachment, recipient, authority and delivery gates as ordinary applications.
